# What's new

What has changed in Steerboard, newest first.

## November 2026

**Microsoft Project's own files.** **Import…** and **New project** read
Project's .mpp file now, as well as the XML that Project saves.

**Safer round trips through Excel.** A workbook exported from Steerboard
remembers the plan as it went out. Import it back and only what was changed in
Excel is offered: changes made in Steerboard in the meantime stay, and anything
changed in both places starts unticked. The workbook also has drop-down lists
for status, type, owner and workstream.

**Import and export, with Excel, Microsoft Project and Jira.**
**Import and export** is in the bar at the top of every project. Export the
plan to Excel, change it there and import it back: each row updates the work
it came from, and you see what's new and what changes, from what to what,
before anything does. CSV files and Microsoft Project work the same way, and
an export from Jira can be imported. A new project can start from a file too:
**A file**, under **Start from**. The list's own **Import…** and **Export** buttons have moved
to the bar; pasting rows onto the list works as before.

**Move lines in the list.** In **List**, drag a line up or down by its handle,
or press ↑ or ↓ on the handle. The item moves on the plan too. The list now
reads as the plan does, from the top, row by row.

**The details say each thing once.** The top of an item's details no longer
repeats itself. One line says how it stands against its agreed dates, the
status choice is its stage, and **Dates** holds the dates as planned, the day
it was finished and the dates agreed. The sentences of explanation in the
panel are gone too: the docs have them.

**Seats, said straight.** People's seats line no longer has a link that leads
nowhere. **See who has a seat** lists them where a seat is freed, and a way
to more seats is there only where your account can take one.

**What changed, easier to find.** **Show on the plan** brings what changed
into view, with a chip in the toolbar to step out. An activity that got
longer shows the shape it had, and a line to where it ends now.

**Programmes, as one thing.** Pick a programme from a list, rename it for all
its projects, start a new project in it, and see the table and the timeline a
programme at a time.

**Share, with a message.** **Share** sends your words with it, and the link
opens the view you were on. Who has access can be changed right there.

**Smaller things.** **Import…** takes a CSV file as well as a paste. The
report's **Period** can look back from a milestone or a date. A template can
be made in **Settings**. A paying account sees its last payments there too.

**Details where you are.** A name on Workload or in the report now opens
that item's details beside it, where it used to take you to the plan. The
panel itself reads more evenly: each part's name is in bold, what you change
is in plain type, and the small print is the only smaller text.

**The slides are in Arial.** Every computer with PowerPoint has it, so a
downloaded deck looks the same wherever it's opened, and names are fitted by
how wide their letters are. A deck made in your template keeps the
template's own font where Steerboard knows its widths.

**Programmes.** A project's menu on Projects has **Programme…**: put projects
that belong together in one, and they're listed under its name.

**Templates of your own.** **Save as a template** in a project's menu keeps
its plan to start projects from. They're in **Settings** under **Templates**,
and in **New project**.

**Share.** **Share** now opens one place to share a project with someone, by
name or email address, and to copy a link to the view you're on.

**The report.** Three headed parts, on white. **Period** sets how far it
looks back and ahead, to a milestone if you like. Its filter has **Changed
since**. Every list of work says whose it is.

**Tracking, your way.** **Project settings**, **Tracking**: whether new work
has its dates agreed as it's added, whether delays and overdue work are
tracked, and how they're drawn.

**Smaller things.** Owners on the plan are a label beside each item. What
changed is drawn in each item's own shape, with a line to where it is now.
The list has **Import…** and **Export**. On a phone held on its side, the bars
are one line each and fold away. Buttons are rounded everywhere.

**Subtitles on the films.** What the narrator says is written on the picture
of both films on the home page, so they play with the sound off. Under the
player, **The tour in words** has all of it to read.

**More tools to the side on a phone.** The plan's tools are one row you swipe
sideways. The row now fades at the edge where there's more.

**Each tab says where it is.** The browser's tab is named after the view and
the project that's open, so several Steerboard tabs can be told apart.

**Easier with a screen reader or a contrast theme.** Buttons, activities and
cards are named by what they show, each view has a heading, and the plan
keeps its colours in a Windows contrast theme.

**Dialogs and menus are the browser's own.** They look as they did. A dialog
now holds the page behind it still, and the back gesture on a phone closes it.
A menu opened from a workstream's name stays with the name while you scroll.

**Text follows your text size.** If you've set bigger text on an Android phone
or tablet, or in your browser, Steerboard's text follows it. The plan's own
drawing keeps its size: **Size**, under **Show**, makes it bigger. iPhones
don't pass their text size on to web pages yet.

**Sign-in says what's wrong sooner.** An email address or a password that
can't be right is said under the field as you leave it.

**Menus answer the arrow keys.** In your account's menu, Help and a project's
own menu, the arrows move between the items and Escape closes the menu. And if
you type in Japanese, Chinese or Korean, Enter no longer saves a field while
you're still choosing a word.

**The logo is whole on every view.** On a phone the header read "Steerboa",
and on a tablet less. The mark and the name are one drawing now, and the bar
makes room for it: on a tablet a project's views take a line of their own
under the bar, and on a phone **Send feedback** is in your account menu.

**Nothing cut short on a phone, a tablet or a laptop.** A workstream's menu
could stay see-through on an iPhone. On a tablet the **Show** menu opened off
the screen, and held on its side it cut the plan's name and its status. The
plan's dates were cut mid-word on a laptop. The report's workstream names, the
projects' search field and the sign-in page are fixed on a phone too.

**New tiers and prices.** The tiers on the home page are now Personal, Team
and Business. Personal costs nothing and is for you and three colleagues on
your email domain. Team is for work with clients, who join free. Business adds
every project on one timeline, single sign-on, the audit log and the data
export. A seat on Team is €12 a month billed yearly, on Business €22.
Steerboard is still in early access, so every account has everything and
nothing is charged.

**Fit uses the whole screen.** **Fit** now fits the activities and milestones
and keeps no room for the names beside them, so the plan is drawn bigger, most
of all on a phone. A name beside the last item can run off the edge.

**One rule for corners.** Everything on a project's views is square now: the
plan, the list, the cards, the workload and the report, with the buttons and
fields that sit on them. The bars at the top, an item's details, menus, dialogs
and settings are slightly round, all by the same amount. Before, corners came
in some twenty sizes.

**Moving several looks like moving one.** Several items dragged together now
follow the pointer, each with an outline where it will land. Before, they
jumped a day at a time with no outline.

**The account's subscription.** Settings has a **Subscription** card for
account managers: the account's tier, and how many of its seats, projects and
people are used. Steerboard is in early access, so every account still has
everything and nothing is charged. A new account starts on Early access too,
and the sign-up page says so. The prices that apply once early access ends are
on the home page.

**Contributors and viewers see only what they can do.** A contributor was
shown **New project**, the **People** page and **Make a copy**, and was told
no when they tried. Those are for planners and account managers, and are no
longer shown to anyone else.

**Files keep the project's name.** A project downloaded as a file is saved
under its name as it's written, in any letters: "Prisanalyse for Ørsted", not
"Prisanalyse for _rsted".

**Names stay tidy.** A name is kept on one line and to a sensible length: 200
letters for an activity or a milestone, 120 for a plan. Emptied, it stays the
name it was.

**Escape closes what's open, at once.** A window that was still loading when
it opened (What changed, say) didn't close on Escape. It does now.

**Moving work keeps its percentage.** Work in progress that was past its
agreed end jumped to 99% when it was moved later. Its percentage now stays
what it was, wherever it's moved.

**Linked work stops where it can start.** Work that waits for other work can't
be pulled before it: its start now stops at the first day it can begin, and
its end stays put. In a work package that waits, only the piece you moved is
held; the rest of the package no longer moves with it.

**The plan's name on a laptop.** On a laptop-wide window the plan's toolbar had
grown until the name was cut to a few letters. The buttons now give up their
words first: **Filter** and **Show** are their icons there, and the name reads
again.

**Your words in the report.** Under the status you can now write **Key
messages**, **Decisions needed** and **Risks**, a line each. They go on the
slides right after the status summary. See
[Your words](report/index.md#your-words).

**Issued reports are kept.** Each report you issue is kept as it was. **Past
reports**, on the Report view, lists them with how the status and the finish
have moved, and opens any of them to read, present and download again. See
[Past reports](report/index.md#past-reports).

**Your projects on a timeline.** On **Projects**, **Timeline** shows every
project as a line on one time scale, with its milestones and status, and
**Download slide** makes a PowerPoint slide of it. See
[Your first project](getting-started/first-project.md#find-archive-and-delete-projects).

**What a milestone depends on.** In a milestone's details, **What this depends
on** lights up the linked work leading to it and says which steps have no
room to spare. See [Links between work](plan/links.md#what-a-milestone-depends-on).

**My work.** A new page, beside **Projects**, lists what's yours across every
project: overdue, in progress, starting this week. Start work, move its
progress, finish it and write an update, from your phone. Contributors land
there when they sign in. See [My work](my-work.md).

**Projects as a table.** On **Projects**, choose **Table** to see every
project as a line you can sort, and **Only late or at risk** to see the ones
that need you. A project's status there is now how it stands today. See
[Your first project](getting-started/first-project.md#find-archive-and-delete-projects).

**For you.** The bell in the bar at the top lists what Steerboard has to tell
you: work given to you, updates that name you, your work that's overdue, and
reports that are due. It works whether or not a project sends email. See
[A tour of the views](getting-started/tour.md#the-bar-at-the-top).

**Updates on a piece of work.** Under an item's notes you can now write dated
updates: where it stands and why. The latest one goes beside the item in the
status report when it's overdue or delayed. See
[Updates](plan/details.md#updates).

**What changed since the last report.** The button with two arrows on the
plan lists what moved, was added, finished or agreed since the last report, or
since a day you pick, with who changed it. **Show on the plan** draws where
each moved item was. See [What changed](plan/changes.md).

**A list to type and paste into.** **List**, the new tab beside **Plan**, shows
the plan as a line for each item: type names, dates and owners straight in,
press Enter for the next line, sort by any column, and paste rows copied from
Excel. See [List](list.md).

**A link to the report, without signing in.** A project's owner can turn on a
view-only link for people who only need to read the status report. No account
needed, and you can give it a last day and a passcode. See
[Sharing a project](sharing/sharing.md#a-link-that-opens-without-signing-in).

**Issue and send.** When you issue a report you can tick who gets it by
email: how the plan stands, your key messages and a link to that report. See
[Issue the report](report/index.md#issue-the-report).

**A PDF, and the plan as a picture.** The arrow beside **Download .pptx** now
also saves the slides as a PDF, and the plan as a picture to download or to
copy and paste into an email. See
[Present and download](report/present.md#a-pdf-and-the-plan-as-a-picture).

**Start from a template, or from last year's project.** **New project** now
asks what to start from: a blank plan, one of four templates (a consulting
engagement, a system roll-out, a sourcing project, a programme), or a copy of
one of your own projects with everything a draft again. See
[Your first project](getting-started/first-project.md#start-a-project).

**Find, and a fuller filter.** Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd> on the plan,
type part of a name and press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to go to it. **Filter** now
also shows one person's work, or only what's overdue, delayed, a draft or
done; the rest fades where it is. See
[Find and filter](plan/board.md#find-and-filter).

**Who owns what, on the plan.** Choose **Show** in the plan's toolbar and tick
**Owners**: each item's owner follows its name as initials, and the report's
plan slide does the same. See [names](plan/items.md#names).

**A year or two on one view.** The time scale has a fourth setting,
**Quarters**. And **Fit**, beside it, picks the scale and the size at which
the whole plan shows. The size slider is now under **Show**. See
[The board](plan/board.md#fit-and-size).

**A key to the marks.** Tick **Key** under **Show** for a small list of the
marks your plan is drawn with and what each is called. See
[The marks](reference/marks.md).

**One set of words for where work stands.** It's **Draft**, **Planned**,
**In progress** and **Done** everywhere: on the cards, in the details panel
and in the menus. The cards' first and third columns were called Identified
and Started. A piece of work is an activity everywhere (**New activity** said
action), and work planned later than agreed is **Delayed** everywhere (the
report said slipped).

**Help in one place.** The question mark at the top is now a menu: the docs
for the view you're on, the keyboard shortcuts (press <kbd>?</kbd> anywhere),
this page, and feedback. A new, empty plan shows three first steps at the
bottom right of the board.

**Smaller things.** The button for a project's settings says **Settings**.
The **Today** button keeps its name on a laptop. Rows that aren't in a
workstream are named **General** on the plan, as they are in the report.
