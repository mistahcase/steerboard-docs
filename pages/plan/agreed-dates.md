# Agreed dates and delays

Steerboard keeps the dates you agreed with the client apart from the dates
you're planning to now. That's how it can tell, by itself, what has slipped.

## Drafts

Everything starts as a draft: pencilled in, drawn white with a dashed outline.
Move it as much as you like. A draft is never late.

## Agree dates

When the client agrees, agree the dates:

- Right-click a draft and choose **Agree dates**.
- Or use **Agree** in the [details panel](details.md).
- Or select several drafts and agree them together.
- On a group's bracket, **Agree dates for everything inside**.
- On the [cards](../cards.md), drag a card from **Identified** to **Planned**.

The item turns solid, and its current dates are kept as its agreed dates. You
can still move it freely; the agreed dates stay put.

## How a delay shows

Once work has agreed dates, moving its end later shows as a delay, counted in
working days ("+3 days").

| How it stands | What you see |
|---|---|
| On plan | Solid navy, and green for what's done. |
| Delayed: planned to end later than agreed, but not due yet | Past its agreed end, the boat goes on in light blue with an amber outline. |
| Overdue: past its agreed end and not done | The light blue part is outlined in red, and grows to today, day by day, until it's done. |
| Finished late | Green all over, ending on the day it was finished. Late by how many days shows in the details panel and the report. |

When work has moved from its agreed dates, a thin grey bar with end ticks
under it shows where it was agreed: the agreed dates, left behind. A moved
milestone leaves a hollow diamond.

A group's status, and a workstream's dot, is the worst of the agreed work
inside it.

## Change agreed dates

Agreed dates are never undone by moving the work. When the scope genuinely
changes, change them: right-click the work and choose
**Change agreed dates…**, or use the button in the details panel. You can give
a reason. The change, and the reason, are kept in the item's history, and the
report shows them.
